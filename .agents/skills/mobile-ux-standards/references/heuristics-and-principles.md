# UX heuristics and principles

Review evidence against these questions:

1. Is system status visible and truthful?
2. Do labels and sequences match the user’s real-world task?
3. Can users cancel, back out, or recover without losing safe work?
4. Are the same actions and states presented consistently?
5. Does the interface prevent likely errors and duplicate actions?
6. Are options and required information visible rather than dependent on memory?
7. Are frequent and returning journeys efficient without hiding the standard path?
8. Is critical information prioritized without unnecessary noise?
9. Do errors identify the problem and a recovery action in plain language?
10. Is contextual help available when the task genuinely needs it?

Classify findings by observable evidence and task impact. Avoid inventing user research; label assumptions and untested risks.
